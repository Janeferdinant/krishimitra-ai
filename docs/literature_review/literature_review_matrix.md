## 1. Introduction and Review Scope

Agricultural commodity price forecasting and market intelligence present high non-linearity, strong seasonality, weather dependency, and market friction effects. To establish a rigorous research foundation for **KrishiMitra AI**, 32 high-impact, peer-reviewed publications and institutional reports were analyzed. The review covers the 4 problem-canvas reference benchmarks (UAS Bangalore, TNAU/RKVY, Procedia CS Arecanuts in Kerala, JSRR Black Pepper in Kerala), complemented by recent 2021–2026 Q1/Q2 literature across IEEE, Elsevier, Springer, and agricultural economics repositories.

## 3. Summary of Key Methodological Insights

1. **Model Progression:**
   - Classical statistical baselines (Naive, Seasonal Naive, Holt-Winters, ARIMA/SARIMA) capture macro linear seasonality but fail during abrupt market shocks and volatile weather events.
   - Non-linear machine learning models (Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM) consistently achieve top performance on tabular time-series features (lags, rolling stats, calendar harmonics) with high computational efficiency.
   - Deep learning architectures (LSTM, BiLSTM, Temporal Attention) capture long-term sequential memory but require substantial data volume and regularization.
   - **Hybrid Architectures (ARIMA + XGBoost residual modeling)** combine the theoretical elegance of linear stationary time-series modeling with the non-linear feature capture of gradient boosted trees, yielding the lowest composite RMSE across literature.

2. **Feature Integration:**
   - Exogenous features—specifically **market arrival volumes** (inverse price elasticity) and **meteorological anomalies** (rainfall accumulation and temperature extremes)—provide critical turning-point predictive power (Sengupta et al., 2023; Kumar et al., 2023).

3. **Explainability & Decision Support:**
   - SHAP (Shapley Additive exPlanations) provides mathematically sound feature attribution, but must be translated into farmer-accessible natural language advice (e.g., *"Expected price increase driven by 35% decline in regional arrivals and favorable harvest timing"*).
